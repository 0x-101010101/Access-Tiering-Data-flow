Zanzibar: Google’s Consistent, Global Authorization System
https://www.usenix.org/conference/atc19/presentation/pang Zanzibar

Zanzibar models authorization through relationships between users, groups, and resources—commonly called relationship-based access control, or ReBAC. Permissions can follow indirect relationships, such as a user belonging to a group that has access to a document. “Graph-based authorization” is therefore the useful search term;

For example, conceptually:

Alice ──member──▶ Engineering ──viewer──▶ Document
An access check can determine that Alice may view the document through her group membership, rather than requiring an explicit Alice-to-document grant.wikipedia
What made the paper important
The contribution is not just representing permissions as a graph: it is evaluating those permissions at global scale while preserving consistency between permission changes and changes to protected content. Google reported trillions of ACLs, millions of authorization requests per second, under 10 ms latency at the 95th percentile, and greater than 99.999% availability over three years of production use. Services using it included Drive, Calendar, Maps, Photos, and YouTube. These are figures reported in the 2019 paper, not current measurements.
