::: notes
The foundation is in place, and it gives us two qualities at once: secure by design, and defence in depth. Secure by design is the foundation itself. Sensitivity is named in any service, a role applies the rules for how that data is managed, a service with no tier cannot ship, and a new field inherits the safe default, so nothing is exposed by accident. Iteration on those two is the organisational framework for Intents. Defence in depth is the layers on top. The first layer is an audit of sessions, so a compromised laptop is visible when the person says they were not the one acting. The second layer, for the people at the top, is a hardware key, so a compromised computer is limited in what it can do and for how long. The key is one layer inside that pair, and it is said with the cut: a touch proves a person was there, and a key with no screen does not prove what they approved. This is where the long-term work starts.

**More:**

Secure by design is the foundation; defence in depth is the layers on it. Keep the key inside the pair, never as the headline. Down is the cut on the key. A touch can sign a different payload from the one on the screen, and the false sense of security is the dangerous part.
:::

## The cut on the key {#v3--cut .concept}

<p class="depth">A little more</p>

- A hardware key is better to have. It is a stepping stone. It is not the finish line. People hear the key and stop, as if the company is already protected.
- The structural gap: a standard key has no display of what is being approved. A touch proves a person was there. It does not prove what they approved.
- On a compromised laptop, the program that talks to the key can be swapped. The screen shows the expected request. The key signs another payload. A second touch can be prompted as if the first failed. People touch again, and the screen still looks fine.
- The false sense of security is the dangerous part. People drop their guard because they touched the key.
- Still open: a second device that shows the exact action and approves it. A second person who can see the flow and pause it. Rate limits sit with that person.
