## The cut on the key {#v3--cut .concept}

<p class="depth">A little more</p>

- A hardware key is better to have. It is a stepping stone. It is not the finish line. People hear the key and stop, as if the company is already protected.
- The structural gap: a standard key has no display of what is being approved. A touch proves a person was there. It does not prove what they approved.
- On a compromised laptop, the program that talks to the key can be swapped. The screen shows the expected request. The key signs another payload. A second touch can be prompted as if the first failed. People touch again, and the screen still looks fine.
- The false sense of security is the dangerous part. People drop their guard because they touched the key.
- Still open: a second device that shows the exact action and approves it. A second person who can see the flow and pause it. Rate limits sit with that person.
