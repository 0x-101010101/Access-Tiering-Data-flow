## A hacked laptop meets stops {#for-them--stops .vision}

<p class="depth">A little more</p>

- A stolen laptop finds the sensitive window closed. The grant expires on its own.
- A rate limit caps what one window can take. A second person, told when the window opens, can see an unusual flow and pause it.
- A hardware key is worth having. It is a stepping stone. A touch proves a person was there. It does not prove what they approved. The key has no screen.
- On a laptop that is already compromised, the program that talks to the key can lie about the payload, and a second touch can be prompted as if the first failed. An unexpected second touch is a stop.
- The record is the other half of the same incident. It holds the payload that was actually approved, so we can name the users who were in what was taken.

::: notes
Say the stops and the key caveat in the same breath. The window expires, the rate limit caps it, a second person can pause it. The key is better than nothing. A touch is not what was approved. The record is how we name who to warn.
:::

## Intents, and AML {#for-them--predicates .vision}

<p class="depth mid">More</p>

- A liquor store checks that you are old enough. It does not take the passport home. A predicate is that move. The answer comes back. The raw values do not.
- Confidential intents use it to check a fact about an intent without opening the intent.
- The legal platform uses it for AML and KYT. A case officer asks the question and gets the result. A lawyer with a court order gets one case, for a fixed time.
- Legal exposure is its own axis. It is not a level on the financial scale.
- If our own people cannot see the data, a person sitting on a stolen laptop cannot see it either.

::: notes
Two jobs, one move. Confidential intents stay closed while a fact is checked. AML and KYT on the legal platform get the result, and a court order is one case for a fixed time. Legal is its own axis. Data we cannot see is data a stolen laptop cannot see.
:::
